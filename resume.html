Resume Preparation

Learn how to create a clear, professional and job-ready resume as a student or fresher.

1. Introduction to Resume Preparation

A resume is a document that presents a person's education, skills, projects, experience and achievements. It helps employers understand a candidate's qualifications and suitability for a job.

For students and freshers, a resume is especially important because it provides an opportunity to highlight academic knowledge, technical skills, projects, certifications and other relevant activities.

Important: A good resume should be clear, accurate, simple and relevant to the job for which you are applying.

What a Resume Should Show

  • Who you are
  • What you have studied
  • What technical skills you have
  • What projects you have completed
  • What certifications you have earned
  • What achievements you have
  • What value you can bring to a company

2. Importance of a Resume

A resume is often one of the first documents reviewed by a recruiter. A well-organized resume can make it easier for recruiters to understand a candidate's background.

Why Is a Resume Important?

  1. It introduces you to the employer.
  2. It summarizes your education and skills.
  3. It highlights your projects and experience.
  4. It shows your certifications and achievements.
  5. It helps recruiters compare candidates.
  6. It provides topics for interview questions.

Resume for Students and Freshers

Students who do not have professional experience can focus on their education, academic projects, internships, technical skills, certifications, competitions and achievements.

Tip: Lack of work experience does not mean you cannot create a strong resume. Highlight your projects, skills and learning.

3. Basic Resume Structure

A resume should be organized into clear sections so that important information can be found easily.

Recommended Resume Sections

1. Name and Contact Details 2. Career Summary 3. Education 4. Technical Skills 5. Projects 6. Internship / Experience 7. Certifications 8. Achievements 9. Additional Activities

Simple Resume Flow

Personal Information ↓ Career Summary ↓ Education ↓ Technical Skills ↓ Projects ↓ Experience / Internship ↓ Certifications ↓ Achievements

The order can be adjusted depending on the candidate's background and the requirements of the job.

4. Contact Details

Contact information allows recruiters to communicate with candidates about interviews, assessments and job opportunities.

Details You Can Include

  • Full name
  • Professional email address
  • Phone number
  • City and state
  • LinkedIn profile
  • GitHub profile, if relevant
  • Portfolio website, if available

Example

Your Name Email: yourname@example.com Phone: +91 XXXXX XXXXX Location: Chennai, India LinkedIn: linkedin.com/in/yourname GitHub: github.com/yourusername
Privacy Tip: Share only the contact information necessary for professional communication. Avoid adding sensitive personal information.

5. Career Summary

A career summary is a short introduction that describes your skills, education and the type of opportunity you are looking for.

For freshers, the summary should focus on their technical knowledge, willingness to learn, problem-solving ability and career interests.

Example for a Computer Science Fresher

A motivated Computer Science student with knowledge of programming, data structures, databases and web technologies. Interested in software development and eager to apply technical and problem-solving skills in a professional environment.

Tips for Writing a Career Summary

  • Keep it short and clear.
  • Mention relevant technical skills.
  • Focus on your strengths.
  • Match the summary with the job role.
  • Avoid unnecessary personal information.
  • Use simple and professional language.

6. Education

The education section provides information about your academic qualifications. Students and freshers should clearly mention their degree, institution and academic details.

Recommended Information

  • Degree or course name
  • College or university name
  • Specialization
  • Year of study or graduation
  • CGPA or percentage, when relevant

Example

Qualification Institution Year Result
B.Tech Computer Science ABC University 2026 8.5 CGPA
Higher Secondary ABC School 2022 90%
Secondary School ABC School 2020 92%
Tip: Always enter your actual academic information. Do not add incorrect marks, qualifications or institutions.

7. Technical Skills

The technical skills section shows the technologies, programming languages and tools that you know.

Examples of Technical Skills

  • Java
  • Python
  • C and C++
  • HTML and CSS
  • JavaScript
  • SQL
  • DBMS
  • Data Structures and Algorithms
  • Git and GitHub
  • Web Development

How to Organize Skills

Programming: Java, Python, C++ Web Technologies: HTML, CSS, JavaScript Database: SQL, DBMS Tools: Git, GitHub, VS Code

Only include skills that you actually understand and can discuss during an interview.

8. Projects

Projects demonstrate how you have applied your knowledge to solve practical problems. They are particularly useful for students and freshers who have limited professional experience.

What to Include for Each Project

  • Project title
  • Short description
  • Technologies used
  • Your contribution
  • Important features
  • Project link, if available

Example Project Description

Student Learning Platform

Developed a web-based learning platform that provides students with study resources, coding practice, quizzes and career preparation materials.

Technologies: HTML, CSS, JavaScript

Project Writing Tips

  1. Explain what problem the project solves.
  2. Mention the technologies used.
  3. Describe important features.
  4. Explain your contribution clearly.
  5. Add a working project link when appropriate.
Important: Be prepared to explain every project mentioned on your resume during the interview.

9. Experience and Internships

Experience can include internships, part-time work, freelance projects, volunteering or other relevant professional activities.

Students who do not have work experience can highlight internships, academic projects and practical learning experiences.

What to Include

  • Organization or company name
  • Job title or internship role
  • Duration
  • Responsibilities
  • Skills used
  • Important achievements

Example

Web Development Intern

ABC Technologies | June 2025 – July 2025

  • Worked on basic web development tasks.
  • Created responsive web pages using HTML and CSS.
  • Practiced JavaScript for interactive features.
  • Collaborated with team members on project tasks.
For Freshers: If you do not have professional experience, focus on projects, internships, academic work and relevant skills.

10. Certifications

Certifications can demonstrate that you have completed additional learning in a particular subject or technology.

Examples

  • Java Programming Certification
  • Python Programming Certification
  • Web Development Certification
  • Database and SQL Certification
  • Data Structures Certification
  • Cloud Computing Certification
  • Artificial Intelligence Certification
  • Machine Learning Certification

Certification Format

Certification Name Issuing Organization Year Credential Link (if available)

Include certifications that are relevant to your education, skills or target job role.

Important: Do not list certifications that you have not actually completed.

11. Achievements

The achievements section can highlight academic, technical and extracurricular accomplishments.

Examples of Achievements

  • Won a coding competition.
  • Participated in a hackathon.
  • Completed a technical project.
  • Received an academic award.
  • Participated in technical events.
  • Completed relevant workshops.
  • Earned recognition for a project.

How to Write Achievements

Write achievements clearly and mention the result whenever possible.

Good: Secured first place in a college-level coding competition. Better: Secured first place among participating students in a college-level programming competition.
Tip: Include genuine achievements that you can explain if asked during an interview.

12. Resume Formatting

Good formatting makes a resume easier for recruiters to read and understand.

Formatting Guidelines

  • Use a clean and professional layout.
  • Use clear section headings.
  • Keep font sizes consistent.
  • Use bullet points for important information.
  • Maintain proper spacing.
  • Check spelling and grammar.
  • Keep contact details easy to find.
  • Use consistent formatting throughout the document.

Recommended Resume Order

NAME Contact Information Career Summary Education Technical Skills Projects Experience / Internship Certifications Achievements

Resume Length

The appropriate resume length depends on the candidate's experience and the requirements of the job. Students and freshers should focus on relevant information rather than adding unnecessary details.

Tip: Quality and relevance are more important than filling the resume with unnecessary information.

14. ATS-Friendly Resume

An Applicant Tracking System (ATS) is software that some organizations use to help manage and screen job applications. An ATS-friendly resume uses clear formatting and relevant information that is easy for both software and recruiters to process.

Tips for an ATS-Friendly Resume

  • Use clear section headings.
  • Use simple and readable formatting.
  • Include relevant keywords from the job description.
  • Clearly mention your technical skills.
  • Use standard job titles and section names.
  • Avoid unnecessary graphics and decorative elements.
  • Check the resume for spelling and grammar errors.
  • Use consistent formatting throughout the document.

Important Keywords

Keywords should be included naturally when they accurately describe your skills and experience.

Example: Job Requirement: Java, SQL, Data Structures, Git Relevant Resume Skills: Java SQL Data Structures and Algorithms Git and GitHub
Important: Never add skills that you do not actually know just to match keywords. You should be able to explain every skill mentioned in your resume.

13. Common Resume Mistakes

Small mistakes can make a resume difficult to read or may create a poor impression. Review your resume carefully before submitting it.

Mistakes to Avoid

  • Spelling and grammar mistakes.
  • Incorrect contact information.
  • Adding false information.
  • Using an unprofessional email address.
  • Adding irrelevant personal information.
  • Using too many colors or decorative elements.
  • Writing long paragraphs instead of clear bullet points.
  • Listing skills without knowing them properly.
  • Adding incomplete project descriptions.
  • Not checking the resume before submitting it.

Resume Checklist

  1. Check your name and contact details.
  2. Check your education information.
  3. Review your technical skills.
  4. Check project descriptions.
  5. Verify certifications and achievements.
  6. Check spelling and grammar.
  7. Remove unnecessary information.
  8. Make sure all information is truthful.
  9. Save the final resume in an appropriate format.

15. Frequently Asked Questions

1. What is a resume?

A resume is a document that summarizes a person's education, skills, projects, experience and achievements for professional opportunities.

2. What should a fresher include in a resume?

Freshers can include their education, technical skills, projects, internships, certifications, achievements and relevant extracurricular activities.

3. How many pages should a resume have?

Resume length depends on the candidate's experience and the job requirements. Freshers should focus on relevant information and avoid unnecessary content.

4. Should students include projects?

Yes. Projects can demonstrate practical knowledge and show how students have applied their technical skills.

5. Should I include my photo on my resume?

Whether a photo is appropriate depends on the country, industry and employer requirements. Follow the instructions given in the job application.

6. What is an ATS-friendly resume?

An ATS-friendly resume uses clear structure, readable formatting and relevant information so that it can be processed more easily by applicant tracking systems and recruiters.

7. Should I mention my GitHub profile?

If you have relevant programming projects or open-source work, a professional GitHub profile can be useful to include.

8. Can I use the same resume for every job?

It is better to review and adjust your resume for each job so that the most relevant skills, projects and experience are clearly presented.

9. Should I add skills that I am currently learning?

You can mention a technology you are currently learning if you clearly describe your level of knowledge. Do not present beginner knowledge as advanced expertise.

10. Why is proofreading important?

Proofreading helps identify spelling mistakes, grammar errors, incorrect dates and formatting problems before the resume is submitted.

Conclusion

A good resume clearly presents your education, skills, projects, experience and achievements. Students and freshers can create a strong resume by focusing on relevant information and presenting it in a simple and professional format.

Keep your resume accurate, easy to read and relevant to the job you are applying for. Review it regularly as you gain new skills, complete projects or earn certifications.

Remember: Your resume should represent your real skills and experience. Keep learning, keep improving and keep your resume updated.
